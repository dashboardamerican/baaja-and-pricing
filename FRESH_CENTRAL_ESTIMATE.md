# BAAJA: $50-100 billion in additional transmission payments

Transmission payments, 2027-2056 | October 31, 2026

[Download the two-page brief and methodology](baaja-brief-and-methodology.pdf) · [Read the methodology online](analysis/fresh-central-2026-10-31/METHODOLOGY_PAGE.md)

## What the bill would raise

We estimate that BAAJA would require data centers to pay an additional $56 billion for transmission, reducing other customers' bills by the same amount. It covers data centers added in 2027-2056, with charges starting in 2030. All figures are in 2026 dollars, without discounting. Selected cases yield $50-100 billion; other outcomes remain plausible. [1, 3]

The bill requires data centers to pay for their assigned upgrades and their share of the existing grid. Utilities must subtract the upgrade payments from the costs recovered through shared transmission rates. With the same investment and electricity demand, each extra dollar paid by data centers is a dollar other customers no longer owe. [1]

## What drives the estimate

We use LBNL's projection of 649 TWh of data-center electricity use in 2030, then assume 3% annual growth through 2040 and 1.5% thereafter. We assume 80% load factor, 75% of new load covered by the bill, and $650,000/MW of assigned upgrades, rising 1% a year above inflation. Data centers already fund 35% of upgrade capital in the baseline. Capital is recovered over 40 years at 5% real financing, plus annual operating costs of 1.5% of capital. Existing-grid charges use PJM tariffs and a national starting estimate of $95,100/MW-year. Upgrade costs, coverage and existing funding are assumptions, not measured national averages. [2-4]

If data centers already fund 50% of upgrade capital, additional payments fall to $47 billion. Faster demand growth and $750,000/MW of upgrades raise them to $103 billion. These cases give the rounded $50-100 billion range. Other assumptions give lower results: covering only data centers added from 2030 yields $35 billion; using $300,000/MW of upgrades yields $26 billion. [3]

## How it compares with the transmission tax credit

JCT estimated that the transmission investment tax credit in the House-passed 2021 Build Back Better bill would cost the federal government $11.279 billion over FY2022-2031. Converting each year to 2026 dollars at 2.5% inflation and repeating that ten-year schedule three times gives $32 billion over 30 years. This extension is our calculation; JCT did not score a 30-year program. [3, 5]

BAAJA's $56 billion central estimate is 1.8 times that amount. The $50-100 billion range is 1.6-3.2 times as large. This compares customer bill relief with the tax credit's federal budget cost, not the policies' economic benefits.

## How much is above cost of service?

Using the larger of ordinary payments or incremental service cost as the benchmark, BAAJA payments are $55.6 billion higher. This result depends on how cost of service is defined. Counting a full share of the existing grid as well as assigned upgrades leaves no excess under this model. Payments exceed incremental cost alone by $145 billion, but $89 billion of that is already paid in the baseline. Only the additional payments measure what the bill changes. [3]
