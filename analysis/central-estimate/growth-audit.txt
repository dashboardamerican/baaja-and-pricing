BILLING PEAK: MATERIAL ISSUE
80% load factor was requested by user; retain it as the visible conversion convention, but calibrate a separate effective-billing multiplier transparently. EPRI documents measured own-peak load factors94% for a studied hyperscale facility and88%average for studied colocation facilities. Relative to E/(8760×0.8), these imply multipliers0.851–0.909. A selected0.90 central multiplier is equivalent to88.89%effective load factor. Sensitivities0.8/0.9/1.0 cover this issue without assuming measured national tariff peaks.

EPRI live dashboard medium2030 is596.35TWh with70.651GW aggregate state noncoincident peak: implied96.36%load factor and0.830multiplier relative80%conversion. Their narrative specifies this peak is summed calculated state peaks, not utility CP billing determinants. The discrepancy is another reason to avoid treating80%as measured national billing demand. Installed/nameplate capacity utilization is a different quantity; LBNL capacity discussion must not be used to assert that its facility peak is simply nameplate.

Do not apply the multiplier once as a load-factor correction and again as a separate coincidence adjustment unless the mechanisms are independently measured. Minimum contracted billing demand can exceed realized peaks; it needs actual contract data.
