Demand

EPRI 2026 supplies the 2030 endpoints; 300 TWh in 2026 is our selected baseline. From 2031 to 2035, demand grows at the rates in IEA’s global scenarios, then tapers by 2040 to 0.5%, 1.5% or 2.5% a year. Values after 2030 are our extrapolation.

Measures

Baseline:  

Bill relief is what credited AND pricing collects above the selected baseline. Embedded uses ordinary shared-network charges; higher-of uses the greater of those charges and incremental cost. It equals the reduction in other customers’ bills. Paid above incremental cost is everything new load pays above its incremental cost, including payments already collected under the baseline. The measures overlap: do not add them. Paid above incremental cost is not an overcharge above fully allocated cost of service. With an embedded baseline, bill relief can exceed the contribution above incremental cost when the baseline shifts part of incremental cost to others.

Independent Claude review · Regional evidence and model audit: project-cost matches, measured PJM rates, and sensitivity tests. OPC, UCS & related papers.

Assumptions

80% load factor. 75% of new demand above 2026 participates in the modeled grid; the other 25% is excluded from both investment and billing demand. This represents grid participation, not a tariff exemption. 829 GW of incumbent peak, held flat. Charges start in 2030, including 2027–2029 entrants. Eligibility and grandfathering are scenario assumptions. The levelized real carrying charge is 7.33% of capital (40 years, 5% real, 1.5% O&M). The rate is EIA AEO2026 transmission spending of $76.7bn divided by 829 GW, in 2026 dollars. It is a proxy, not a tariff average. Capital remains a selected assumption. The central case assumes no additional payments beyond the selected baseline; existing direct funding and special contracts must be netted out in a project-specific estimate. A tariff need not be called AND to collect those payments. Guarantees of embedded revenue are not automatically additional recovery.

ITC

JCT scored the 2021 Build Back Better transmission ITC at $11.3bn over FY2022–2031. We convert it to 2026 dollars and repeat it three times: $31.7bn. Holding it at its final scored level instead gives $53bn. This is not an official score. Bill relief is a private transfer; the ITC is a federal cost.
